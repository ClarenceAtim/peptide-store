BPC-157: what the research looks at

9 November 2026

BPC-157 is one of the most talked-about peptides in research — and one of the most misunderstood. It is worth getting clear on what it actually is, and what the published research does and does not show.

What BPC-157 is

BPC-157 is a pentadecapeptide: a chain of 15 amino acids. It is a fragment of a larger protein known as Body Protection Compound, which is found in human gastric juice. The full sequence was first described by researchers in Croatia in the 1990s, and most of the published work since then has come from that research group.

What research has looked at

Almost all of the published work on BPC-157 is in animal models — rats, mostly — and in cell studies. Researchers have looked at:

  • Tendon, ligament and muscle tissue in injury models, where studies measured healing markers after a controlled injury.
  • Gut tissue, including models of stomach and intestinal damage.
  • Blood-vessel formation (angiogenesis) and collagen production in the tissues studied.

This is preclinical research. It describes what happened in animals and in cells under laboratory conditions. It is not the same as evidence in people, and there are no large human clinical trials of BPC-157.

What BPC-157 is not

BPC-157 is not an approved medicine anywhere in the world. It is not a dietary supplement, and it has no established safety profile in humans — because it has never been through the clinical testing that medicines go through. Anyone selling it as a treatment, or describing what it does in the human body, is going beyond the evidence. On this store it is listed strictly as a research compound, for laboratory research use only.

How it is supplied for research

For laboratory research, BPC-157 is supplied as a freeze-dried powder in sealed vials. This store lists BPC-157 5 mg, BPC-157 10 mg and BPC-157 20 mg vials, as well as BPC-157 capsules.
