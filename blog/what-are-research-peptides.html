What are research peptides?

9 November 2026

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins. The usual dividing line is length: a chain of fewer than about 50 amino acids is called a peptide, and longer chains are proteins. Insulin, for example, is a peptide of 51 amino acids, sitting right on that border.

In the body, peptides work as signalling molecules. Hormones, neurotransmitters and growth factors are peptides, or act through them. That is why laboratories study them: a peptide can switch a specific biological process on or off, which makes it a precise tool for research into how the body works.

How research peptides are supplied

Research peptides are normally supplied as a freeze-dried (lyophilised) powder in a sealed glass vial. In the laboratory the powder is dissolved in sterile water before use. Two documents matter more than anything else:

  • Purity, measured by HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography). Reputable suppliers list a purity figure, usually above 98%.
  • Identity, confirmed by mass spectrometry, which checks that the chain of amino acids in the vial is the one on the label.

Both are summarised in a certificate of analysis (COA) from an independent laboratory. Vials should be kept cold, dry and away from light, and handled only in a laboratory setting.

Research use only

Compounds sold for laboratory research are not medicines. Some peptides live in two worlds: semaglutide, for instance, is a prescribed medicine for type 2 diabetes and weight management, and it is also supplied as a research compound. Others, such as BPC-157, are not approved medicines anywhere. Buying a research compound is not the same as buying a medicine, and research materials are not for human or animal use. They are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.

What to look for in a supplier

Before ordering research peptides, check that the supplier lists a certificate of analysis, states purity clearly, labels vial sizes plainly, and answers questions. The peptide families researchers study

Research falls into a few broad areas, which match the ranges in this store:

  • Healing — peptides studied in tissue research, such as BPC-157 and TB-500.
  • Growth / Secretagogues — peptides studied for growth-hormone signalling, such as CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin.
  • Cognitive — peptides studied in brain and nervous-system research, such as Semax and Selank.
  • Weight Loss — GLP-1 peptides studied in metabolic research, such as semaglutide and tirzepatide.
  • Cellular — peptides studied in cell-energy research, such as NAD+ and MOTS-c.
  • Tanning — peptides studied for melanin signalling, such as Melanotan II.
