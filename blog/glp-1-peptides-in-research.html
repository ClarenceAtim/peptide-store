GLP-1 peptides in research: semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide

9 November 2026

GLP-1 peptides are among the most studied compounds in metabolic research today. GLP-1 — glucagon-like peptide-1 — is a hormone the gut releases after eating. It signals fullness, slows how fast the stomach empties, and helps regulate blood sugar. Compounds that mimic it, called GLP-1 receptor agonists, are now widely prescribed medicines. They are also studied as laboratory research compounds, which is where the three names in this post come in.

Semaglutide

Semaglutide is the best-known GLP-1 receptor agonist. It is approved as a prescribed medicine for type 2 diabetes and for weight management in many countries, and it is the compound behind the most-discussed drugs in this class. In research it is supplied as a laboratory compound: this store lists Semaglutide 5 mg vials, along with other sizes, in the Weight Loss range.

Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide acts on two receptors instead of one: GLP-1 and GIP, another gut hormone involved in blood-sugar regulation. It is also approved as a prescribed medicine for type 2 diabetes and for weight management. Much of the current research compares dual action with GLP-1 alone — how the two signals behave together, and where the differences show up. This store lists Tirzepatide 5 mg vials and other sizes.

Retatrutide

Retatrutide goes one step further: it acts on three receptors — GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. It is still investigational, in clinical trials, and not approved as a medicine anywhere. That makes it one of the most closely watched compounds in metabolic research, and also the one with the least settled evidence. This store lists Retatrutide 5 mg vials and other sizes for laboratory research.

Why researchers study them

Research on these compounds centres on appetite signalling, blood-sugar regulation and body-weight research — and on how the single, dual and triple receptor combinations behave differently. That comparison is the point of studying all three side by side, rather than any one alone.

One important distinction: the approved medicines and the research compounds are the same molecules, but they are not the same product. A prescribed medicine comes with a doctor, a pharmacy and a tested dosing schedule. A research compound is a laboratory material, supplied for research use only — not for human or animal use, and not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
